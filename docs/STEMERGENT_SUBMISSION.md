## 1. Title and one line
**Ghost Touch: feel diagrams you cannot see.**  
Take a photo, screenshot or PDF page of a school diagram, and a blind or low-vision student can explore it by touch on an ordinary Android phone: the phone buzzes on lines and at corners, speaks the labels, plays a rising tone along a graph, and answers spoken questions about the picture.

## 2. The problem
Diagrams are how much of STEM is taught: triangles in geometry, curves in algebra, bar charts in data handling. For a blind or low-vision student, a diagram is a gap on the page. Tactile (raised-line) graphics fix this, but they are slow and costly to produce (often taking weeks via specialist embossers or swell paper), so most diagrams never reach the student on the day they are taught. Refreshable tactile displays exist but cost thousands of pounds (e.g. Dot Pad at ~£6,500+). Image-description apps turn a picture into a paragraph, but a paragraph cannot convey spatial intuition—feeling where a line turns, where two curves intersect, or how steep a slope is.

Most students already own a budget Android smartphone, and phones have a vibration motor, speakers, a touchscreen, and a microphone. Ghost Touch turns that commodity hardware into an interactive tactile diagram reader at zero additional hardware cost.

## 3. Who it helps
Blind and low-vision school students, and the teachers and parents who prepare STEM materials for them. The target device is a budget Android phone running Chrome; students who rely on TalkBack are a first-class consideration, not an afterthought.

## 4. The STEM connection
The application is engineered specifically for diagrams standard to STEM curricula:
- **Geometry:** Labelled vertices, line segments, right angles, side lengths, and polylines. Tracing an edge gives a continuous vibration buzz; at a corner the phone gives a double pulse and speaks the corner's label, for example "C", and pauses line buzz for 450 ms so the corner stands out clearly.
- **Function Graphs (Theremin Sonification):** Mathematical curves (e.g. sine waves, parabolas) where visual height is sonified using Web Audio as a continuous theremin tone (200 Hz to 1000 Hz). As the student traces upward, the pitch rises; as the curve descends, the pitch drops.
- **Bar Charts:** Discrete bars where pitch and value readout communicate comparative heights and categories.
- **Spoken STEM Q&A (Ask):** Students can speak questions such as "How long is AB?", "Where is the right angle?", or "What is the peak?", receiving short, grounded spoken answers derived strictly from detected diagram geometry and facts, backed by deterministic mathematical extraction.

## 5. How it works
1. **Intake & Multimodal Vision:** Camera photo, image file, or the first page of a PDF (rendered client-side via lazy-loaded PDF.js). Three built-in sample diagrams (Right triangle, Sine wave, Bar chart) run 100% locally with zero network requests. The backend sends the image through the provider chain: Google Gemini is the default in codebase architecture, while production deployment on Render sets OpenAI first (`VISION_PROVIDERS: openai,gemini,nebius`). The extraction prompt instructs models to use pedagogical vocabulary ("hypotenuse", "vertex B") and rejects purely visual descriptions ("blue polygon"). All coordinates are normalized to [0, 1]. Every model response is strictly validated against a Pydantic schema and retried once if invalid.
2. **Deterministic Fallback (Raw Mode):** If AI providers time out, fail, or encounter network errors, classical OpenCV Canny edge detection extracts outlines into normalized segments. The app announces: *"The creature's eyes are blurry, you can still feel the outlines."* The student is never left with an empty screen.
3. **Tactile & Auditory Exploration:** As the student's finger moves across the canvas:
   - Lines vibrate continuously (`vibrate(80)` retriggered every 70 ms).
   - Corners fire a distinct double-pulse (`vibrate([50, 90, 50])`), announce the label, and suppress line buzz for 450 ms.
   - Curves play a continuous sine-wave theremin pitch (200–1000 Hz) mapped to screen height.
   - Lifting the finger instantly silences all vibration and tone.
   These haptic parameters were tuned by hand on the owner's OnePlus Nord CE6 in the haptics prototype (prototypes/haptics-test.html); end-to-end testing of the full app on the phone is still pending.
4. **TalkBack Pass-Through Support:** TalkBack normally consumes single-finger gestures. The TalkBack double-tap-and-hold gesture worked on our prototype on a OnePlus Nord CE6 (docs/research/talkback-spike.md). It has not yet been tested in the full app. The Explore screen announces this gesture upon entry, starts the virtual cursor at the centre with a crisp 25 ms centre reference tick on each hold, and enables leaving via Android Back gesture or an accessible 48×48 px button.
5. **Ask & Semantic Voice Q&A:** Students can tap "Ask" or speak voice commands in Explore ("where is point A", "read title", "repeat"). Semantic questions ("where is the right angle?", "what is the peak?") are resolved deterministically on the backend from diagram geometry without hallucination; non-semantic questions are answered by Gemini/OpenAI backed by deterministic facts lookup. A text input is provided if speech recognition is unavailable.
6. **Teacher Correct Mode & Ghostwriter:** Sighted educators can tap "Correct" to adjust points, edit labels, or delete elements over the original photo underlay, with full undo/redo and live validation against `POST /api/v1/validate`. In Teach mode (`#/teach`), Ghostwriter allows teachers to add new points and join edges via accessible "Add point" and "Add edge" controls (minimum 48×48 px targets, spoken announcements, and spoken undo), capped at 300 points and 300 segments.
7. **Frankenstein Laboratory Theme, Accessibility & Spec Ghost:** High-contrast theme meeting WCAG AAA contrast ratios (≥7:1), Atkinson Hyperlegible typography for low-vision distinction, Controls are tested for 48 px targets; state changes are spoken (see tests/a11y.*), `prefers-reduced-motion` compliance, and localized speech/text in English, Hindi (`hi`), and Gujarati (`gu`) (machine-translated; pending native-speaker review). Spec Ghost checks printed facts against geometry on entry and Ask, speaking localized caveats ("Facts and drawing disagree on {name}. I can't tell which is right.", using localized angle words like "angle C", "कोण C", "ખૂણા C") when inconsistencies are found.
8. **Guide My Finger (Optional / Feature Flagged OFF):** Guide and Share ship in the live app but stay off until the user turns them on in Settings; neither has been tested on a phone. When enabled, provides directional audio/haptic guidance toward diagram elements, peaks, or Ask targets with accelerating proximity pulses, pitch height cues, headphone stereo panning, and spoken directions ("left", "up-right", "here") ending with an arrival buzz. (Requires `VITE_FEATURE_GUIDE=1` and Settings toggle).
9. **Teacher Verify & Share (Optional / Feature Flagged OFF):** Guide and Share ship in the live app but stay off until the user turns them on in Settings; neither has been tested on a phone. Sighted teachers can review diagrams at `#/teach`, sign with their name and note, and generate offline URL fragment hashes (`#s=...`) and QR codes. Displays an honest "Shared by [Teacher], not checked" attribution banner when opened by students via an accessible tap-to-open `ShareGate`. Names and titles are stripped of badge words and checkmarks via prefix sanitization. Verification signifies human teacher review, not cryptographic proof. (Requires `VITE_FEATURE_SHARE=1` and Settings toggle).

The core decision logic is a pure state machine (no browser code), enabling exhaustive property testing; browser APIs live in thin drivers.
