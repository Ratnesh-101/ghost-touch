# User Test Task Sheet & Facilitator Protocol

> **Target Session Length:** ~20 minutes per student  
> **Audience:** Blind and low-vision students (middle school, high school, or college)  
> **Facilitator Materials:**  
> - Primary Android phone (charged $\ge 80\%$, volume at comfortable 75%–80%, vibration enabled in system settings)  
> - Secondary backup Android phone (identically configured)  
> - Observation / scoring sheet and pen  
> - Optional tactile print or baseline verbal description card  
> - Stopwatch / timer  
>
> **Designated Explore Gesture:**  
> - **With TalkBack ON:** Double-tap and **hold** on the second tap, then drag without lifting.  
> - **With TalkBack OFF:** Direct single-finger touch and drag (or double-tap-and-hold drag).  
> - *Relative Cursor Anchor:* The virtual finger starts at the exact centre of the diagram. A subtle 25 ms tick and spoken *"Centre."* confirms entry.  
>
> **Facilitator Note on Language Localization:**  
> Ghost Touch supports English, Hindi, and Gujarati via the Lab settings sheet (*"Settings: English, 1.0×"*). **Hindi and Gujarati interface text and speech synthesis are machine-translated and have not yet been validated by a native speaker.** Participant and teacher feedback on terminology, naturalness, and comprehension is an explicit part of this test.

## Session Time Budget (20 Minutes Total)

| Section | Activity | Time Box |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Welcome & Consent** | Read consent script, verify assent/permissions | 2–3 min |
| **1. Gesture Practice** | Teach the double-tap-and-hold drag gesture from Start to Capture | 2 min |
| **2. Baseline Comparison** | Half the participants do baseline first; half do app first | 2 min |
| **3. Task 1: Triangle Corners** | Locate all 3 corners of the triangle specimen and name them | 3–4 min |
| **4. Task 2: Right Angle** | Identify which corner forms the 90° right angle | 1–2 min |
| **5. Task 3: Sine Graph Peaks** | Trace the wave curve and count the upward peaks | 2–3 min |
| **6. Task 4: Bar Chart** | Identify which vertical bar is the tallest | 2 min |
| **7. Task 5: Voice Question** | Ask one spoken question using the Ask button | 2 min |
| **8. Debrief & Survey** | Spoken 5-question survey, language feedback, and open remarks | 3 min |

## Baseline Protocol (Order Balance)

To measure whether Ghost Touch provides an advantage over standard classroom methods:
- **Group A (Odd Participants - P1, P3...):** Baseline description first, then Ghost Touch prototype.
- **Group B (Even Participants - P2, P4...):** Ghost Touch prototype first, then baseline description.

### Baseline Stimulus (Spoken Description)
**[SPOKEN - Baseline Only]**  
> *"I am going to describe a diagram to you with words: 'There is a right-angled triangle labeled ABC. Corner A is at the top left, corner B is at the bottom left with a 90-degree right angle, and corner C is at the bottom right. The line AB is vertical, BC is horizontal, and AC connects them diagonally.' "*  
> *Ask: "Which corner is the right angle, and where is corner C located?"*  
> *Record: Response accuracy and response latency in seconds.*

## Step-by-Step Facilitator Protocol

### Step 1: Teaching the Gesture (2 Minutes)

**[FACILITATOR INSTRUCTION]**  
Place the phone flat on the desk in front of the student or let them hold it securely. Ensure TalkBack is running. Show the Start screen (featuring the creature mascot and organ triad). The entire screen functions as one large button labelled **"Wake the creature"**.

**[SPOKEN]**  
> *"Ghost Touch is designed as a creature living in a laboratory. Right now we are on the Start screen. Double-tap anywhere to 'Wake the creature'.*  
> *Now we are on the Capture screen. To explore any diagram with TalkBack running, we use a special pass-through gesture:*  
> *You tap twice with one finger, but on the second tap, **keep your finger pressed down against the glass**, then gently slide your finger around.*  
> *When your finger touches down, the app places your cursor right at the center of the screen, and you will hear a small click and the word 'Centre'. As you slide across lines, the phone will buzz continuously. When you hit a corner or label, it will give a sharp double-pulse and speak the name.*  
> *Let's try that touch together once."*

**[FACILITATOR INSTRUCTION]**  
- If invited, gently guide the student's finger: tap-tap (hold) and drag.  
- Count how many attempts it takes until the student performs the gesture independently.

**What to Observe:**
- Does the student release their finger too quickly (triggering a normal TalkBack single click)?
- Did the student hear the "Centre." prompt and feel the 25 ms tick?
- Number of attempts required (Target: $\le 2$ attempts).

---

### Step 2: Task 1 — Find the Corners of the Triangle (3–4 Minutes)

**[FACILITATOR INSTRUCTION]**  
On the Capture screen, swipe to and double-tap the **Right triangle** specimen card (with mini preview). A brief spark arc flashes as the specimen table frame opens in Explore. Confirm Explore screen is active.

**[SPOKEN]**  
> *"On the specimen table right now is a triangle. Using your double-tap-and-hold drag gesture, explore the screen to find all three corners of the triangle. Tell me out loud when you feel a corner and what letter it announces."*

**What to Observe:**
- Does the student search systematically (spiral, grid, or sweeping back and forth)?
- Does the student feel the continuous line buzz before reaching a corner?
- Does the "bzzzz-tap" corner double-pulse and 450 ms quiet period feel distinct from the line vibration?
- Start stopwatch when the student touches the glass; stop when they name the 3rd corner.
- Record corners found (0 to 3) and elapsed seconds.

---

### Step 3: Task 2 — Identify the Right Angle (1–2 Minutes)

**[FACILITATOR INSTRUCTION]**  
Remain on the same triangle diagram in Explore.

**[SPOKEN]**  
> *"One of the corners in this triangle forms a 90-degree right angle. Explore the corners again, and tell me: which corner is the right angle?"*

**What to Observe:**
- Does the student explore the perpendicular lines meeting at corner B?
- Does the app speak "Point B. Right angle." or does the student deduce it from geometry?
- Record: Correct (Yes / No) and elapsed time in seconds.

---

### Step 4: Task 3 — Count the Peaks on the Sine Graph (2–3 Minutes)

**[FACILITATOR INSTRUCTION]**  
Double-tap the **Back** button at top left (or use TalkBack back gesture) to return to Capture. Double-tap the **Sine wave** specimen card. Verify Explore screen is loaded with the sine wave graph.

**[SPOKEN]**  
> *"Now we have a mathematical graph of a curve on the specimen table. As you trace along this curve, you will feel vibration, and you will hear a theremin tone that gets higher in pitch as the curve goes up, and lower in pitch as the curve goes down.*  
> *Explore the curve from left to right, and tell me: how many high peaks does this wave have?"*

**What to Observe:**
- Does the student notice the rising and falling audio pitch (200 Hz to 1000 Hz)?
- Does the audio pitch assist in staying centered on the curve?
- Does the student detect when crossing the axes (app speaks "x-axis" / "y-axis")?
- Record: Peaks counted (Correct: 1 peak above axis on standard sample) and elapsed seconds.

---

### Step 5: Task 4 — Identify the Tallest Bar (2 Minutes)

**[FACILITATOR INSTRUCTION]**  
Return to Capture via Back button. Double-tap the **Bar chart** specimen card. Verify Explore screen is loaded with the bar chart.

**[SPOKEN]**  
> *"Now we have a bar chart with vertical bars representing categories. When your finger touches a bar, the phone will speak the category name and play a tone matching its height.*  
> *Explore the bars across the screen and tell me: which bar is the tallest?"*

**What to Observe:**
- Does the student distinguish between adjacent bars?
- Does the pitch comparison clearly convey which value is greater?
- Record: Correct bar named (Yes / No) and elapsed seconds.

---

### Step 6: Task 5 — Ask One Question by Voice or Text (2 Minutes)

**[FACILITATOR INSTRUCTION]**  
Remain on the diagram. Point out the accessible **Ask** button at the bottom of Explore (or teach the two-finger hold shortcut: hold two fingers on canvas for > 500 ms). Point out the fallback text input box if voice recognition is unavailable.

**[SPOKEN]**  
> *"Ghost Touch has a voice assistant that can answer questions about the diagram you are touching. I am going to help you activate the Ask button.*  
> *When it chirps, ask a question about the diagram, such as: 'How long is side AB?' or 'Which corner has the right angle?' "*

**What to Observe:**
- Does the student activate the Ask control successfully?
- Is the student's question captured clearly without speech overlap?
- Does the assistant answer within 2 sentences using grounded diagram facts?
- If the student asks an unanswerable question, does it speak the safety fallback: *"I don't know from this diagram"*?
- Record: Question asked, answer received, understood (Yes / No).

---

### Step 7: Language Evaluation (Optional / Facilitator Check)

**[FACILITATOR INSTRUCTION]**  
If the student is fluent in Hindi or Gujarati, open the Lab settings sheet using the **"Settings: English, 1.0×"** button on Capture. Select **हिन्दी** (Hindi) or **ગુજરાતી** (Gujarati). Ask the participant to listen to the spoken prompts and review wording.

**[SPOKEN]**  
> *"We have added Hindi and Gujarati translations, which were translated by computer. We would love to know: does the pronunciation sound natural to you, and are the mathematical words clear?"*

**What to Observe:**
- Record any awkward phrasings, pronunciation errors, or confusing terms reported by the student or teacher.

## Troubleshooting & Fail-Safe Contingency Plans

| Failure Scenario | Immediate Action |
|---|---|
| **No Internet / Wi-Fi drops** | Ghost Touch is a Progressive Web App (PWA) with built-in offline specimen cards. **Do not attempt camera capture.** Direct the student to the 3 offline specimen cards (**Right triangle**, **Sine wave**, **Bar chart**) on the Capture screen. All haptics, theremin pitch, and facts run 100% locally in the browser engine. |
| **Vision API timeout / Cloud error** | If capturing a new photo takes >15 s or fails, the app automatically switches to **Raw Mode** with notice: *"The creature's eyes are blurry, you can still feel the outlines."* Inform the student: *"The AI could not read the text labels, so the phone is now vibrating on the visual outlines directly."* Continue testing line tracing. |
| **Phone vibration feels weak or absent** | 1. Check Android Settings > Accessibility > Vibration & haptics > Touch feedback (must be ON at maximum).<br>2. Confirm Chrome has permission for vibration.<br>3. Remind observers that the app has not yet undergone official physical device motor calibration.<br>4. If persistent, switch immediately to the **spare phone**. |
| **TalkBack gesture is frustrating or failing** | 1. Remind the student: *"Remember to keep your finger pressed down against the glass on that second tap before sliding."*<br>2. If TalkBack continues to consume gestures after 3 failed attempts, use the Android accessibility shortcut: **press and hold both volume keys for 3 seconds** to suspend TalkBack, test direct touch exploration, then hold both volume keys again to resume TalkBack. Document this fallback in the notes. |
| **Audio cannot be heard clearly** | Turn on the paired Bluetooth speaker or offer clean over-ear headphones. Ensure device media volume is set to 75%–80%. |
